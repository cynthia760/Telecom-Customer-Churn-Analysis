# 📊 Telecom Customer Churn Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyses customer churn within a telecommunications company to identify the customers most at risk of leaving and the factors contributing to customer loss.

The analysis covers 7,043 customers and examines churn across contract type, internet service, customer tenure, monthly charges, age, data usage and location.

A total of 1,869 customers churned, representing an overall churn rate of 26.54%.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Measure the overall customer churn rate and customer status
- Compare churn across internet type, contract, tenure, charges, age and data usage
- Identify the customer groups most at risk of leaving
- Analyse the major reasons associated with customer loss
- Examine revenue associated with churn
- Translate the findings into practical customer-retention recommendations

## 🧹 Data Cleaning & Preparation

Before conducting the analysis:

- 7,043 unique customer records were validated
- Duplicate customer records were checked
- N/A values were interpreted according to their context
- Age, tenure, monthly charges, data usage and population were grouped into analytical categories
- A churn indicator was created
- Customer totals and churn calculations were validated

## 🔍 Key Findings

### Contract Type
Month-to-month customers recorded the highest churn rate at *45.84%, compared with **10.71%* for one-year contracts and only *2.55%* for two-year contracts.

### Customer Tenure
New customers were particularly vulnerable, recording a *47.44% churn rate. Customers who stayed had an average tenure of approximately **41 months, compared with approximately **18 months* among churned customers.

### Internet Service
Fibre-optic customers recorded the highest churn rate among active internet users at *40.72%, compared with **18.58%* for DSL customers.

### Monthly Charges
Customers in higher monthly-charge groups experienced churn rates of approximately *33%, compared with only **10.72%* among customers in the low-charge category.

### Customer Age
Elderly customers recorded the highest churn rate at *32.55%, compared with **24.45%* for middle-aged customers and *22.68%* for younger customers.

### Data Usage
High-data users recorded a *34.04% churn rate, compared with **22.02%* among low-data users.

### Location
Tank Bus Stop recorded the highest churn rate among the analysed locations at *34.55%, while Isiokpo recorded the lowest at **19.64%*.

### Churn Reasons
Competitor-related churn represented the largest amount of lost customer revenue, accounting for approximately *₦1.69M*.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the telecommunications company should:

- Strengthen onboarding and early support for new customers
- Encourage suitable month-to-month customers to move to longer-term contracts
- Investigate fibre-optic service reliability, pricing, speed and customer support
- Proactively contact customers showing high-risk characteristics before cancellation
- Review the value offered to high-charge and heavy-data customers
- Develop competitive offers based on customer needs rather than relying on blanket discounts
- Prioritise retention efforts in high-churn locations such as Tank Bus Stop and Abuloma

## 📌 Conclusion

The analysis shows that customer churn is concentrated among specific customer groups rather than being evenly distributed across the customer base.

New customers, month-to-month subscribers, fibre users, high-charge customers and heavy-data users showed particularly important churn patterns. Targeted retention strategies, improved customer experience and stronger long-term value propositions could therefore help reduce customer loss.
